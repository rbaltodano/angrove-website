I built Angrove because AI should enhance our humanity, not replace it. Small models now run on a phone, which makes a private place for life’s biggest questions possible. This app is my attempt at that, and it is in active development.

Why I built it

What Angrove
Stands On

Human thought is increasingly scraped, analyzed, and stored by tech companies to sell and train off of. Because of this, the questions that matter most about meaning, existence, and truth, are often too personal to ask openly, so they go unexamined. Angrove is built on three commitments to give them somewhere safe to go.

  1. I

    Privacy

    Built so your questions stay on your phone.

    Angrove is built to run Google’s Gemma 4 model entirely on your iPhone. There’s no server reading your questions and no cloud storing your conversations. In the current development build, saved personal data is encrypted on your device and answers run offline. Release validation is still in progress.

    On-device · Encrypted · Works offline

  2. II

    Performance

    Real Reasoning in Your Pocket

    Grounded in a curated library of texts and cites its sources, so your most difficult questions get careful answers, and it says so when a source is missing rather than inventing a quotation.

    Gemma 4 · Curated library

  3. III

    Personalization

    It Asks Back

    Angrove does more than answer. It prompts you to go deeper and connect the ideas you keep, so your understanding builds over time to facilitate true understanding.

    Insight Tree · Study

A Map of Your Own Thinking

An answer highlights the terms worth keeping. Save one and it becomes an Insight, your Insight Tree grows to hold it, and Study lets you walk around it in 3D.

“What did Aristotle mean by eudaimonia?”

Thought for 22s ›

Aristotle’s whole picture of reality is teleological: everything has a telos, an end built into its nature, and things are understood by grasping what they are for. An acorn’s telos is to become an oak; an eye’s is to see. is the same idea applied to a human life as a whole. If everything has a proper end, what is the proper end of a person? Eudaimonia

Flourishing: the complete, well-lived human life that Aristotle held every action ultimately aims at. Not a feeling of happiness, but a life lived well over its whole length.

The Library

Answers You Can Trace, Works You Can Hear.

Angrove grounds its answers in a Library of primary sources: Scripture, the Church Fathers, Thomas Aquinas, the councils and catechisms, and the classical philosophers and historians. All 51,836 passages are searched on your phone, and the works can be read aloud like an audiobook.

  • Passages, not paraphrase. Answers draw on real passages from the Library, and scripture citations link straight to the text.
  • Reference, not memory. What Angrove cites is looked up in the Library when you ask, not recalled from what the model learned in training.
  • Honest about its limits. When a question depends on a source the Library doesn't hold, Angrove should say so rather than invent a quotation. A phone-sized model can still misquote, so check anything that matters.
  • Read or listen to the whole work. Every source opens in full, and a voice that runs on your phone can read it to you from any word.
